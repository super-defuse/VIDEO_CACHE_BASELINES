\section{Layer-Window Ablation}
\label{sec:app-layer-window}

\textsc{Carnator} applies region-restricted latent updates within a window of middle Transformer
layers and leaves the remaining layers to historical sparse-connectivity reuse. We ablate the width
of that window on Wan2.2-TI2V-5B, whose 30 layers are split into a $10$--$20$ region-update window
(11 layers) and an outer $0$--$9,21$--$29$ complement in every recorded 300-request run.

To make the window the only variable, we fix a single accepted request--cache pair and vary nothing
else: the target prompt (\emph{A cute little lion and a wobbly penguin appear}, seed 20260234) and
its source (\emph{A tiger and a lion are fighting}) are held constant, the Early Signature gate is
disabled so that every arm reuses the same accepted cache, and all arms run through the same harness
as the 300-request table. The pair is the one whose \textsc{Carnator} CLIP-T and VBench-Q sit closest
to the 300-request means, and it exercises the one-noun-substitution case (\emph{lion} shared,
\emph{penguin} for \emph{tiger}). Windows are nested around layer~15, the centre of the deployed
window, with the extra layer of an even count taken on the higher-index side. Because the
region-update and outer sparse-connectivity families both rewrite \texttt{block.forward}, the two
sets must remain disjoint, so widening the window necessarily shrinks outer sparse-connectivity
coverage; the sweep therefore measures the partition between the two reuse mechanisms rather than the
window in isolation. As a control, the 11-layer arm reproduces the recorded arrival-16 row within
$1.4$~s of latency and exactly on quality (CLIP-T $0.2676$, VBench-Q $0.6415$), which validates the
harness accounting and bounds run-to-run variation at the $\sim1$~s level.

Latency decreases with window width throughout the sweep. Five layers already suffice to recover
most of the available saving ($1.70\times$ DiT, $1.44\times$ E2E), and the full 30-layer window
reaches $1.89\times$ and $1.56\times$.\footnote{The all-layers arm exercises a code path that
narrower windows never reach: layer~0 receives half-precision input from the patch embedding, so the
region-update path must promote the block output dtype before writing it back. The promotion is a
dtype fix only and changes no arithmetic.} The entire sweep spans $15.6$~s of DiT latency and
$16.4$~s of E2E latency, i.e.\ about $5\%$ of the dense reference, so the window is a second-order
knob: the dominant saving comes from reuse itself, and the partition only decides how the remaining
layers are spent. Per-layer gains are uneven --- isolated to the reuse stage (40 steps, probe
excluded) the arms range from $133.18$~s to $117.92$~s, yet the 15-to-20-layer step shrinks the reuse
stage by only $0.3$~s, and the E2E differences among the 15-, 20- and 25-layer arms are not ordered
by window width and span $3.1$~s, of the same order as the $1.4$~s by which the 11-layer arm
reproduces the recorded arrival-16 latency. The trend is monotone at the two ends of the sweep, but
single-request granularity does not resolve its interior.

Quality is flat for windows up to 20 layers and degrades beyond. CLIP-T stays within
$0.2617$--$0.2803$, at or above the 300-request mean of $0.2616$, and VBench-Q stays within
$0.6092$--$0.6497$ around its mean of $0.6401$. On this pair the 20-layer arm is the best-quality arm
in the sweep while also being faster than the deployed 11-layer configuration. Past 20 layers the
scores fall, driven by aesthetic quality ($0.5798$ at 20 layers, then $0.5002$ and $0.4237$ at 25
and 30) and subject consistency ($0.8493$, then $0.7986$ and $0.7539$), whereas imaging quality does
not degrade ($0.7487$, $0.7552$, $0.7744$). The widest window thus makes the reuse-induced losses
visible in exactly the dimensions that depend on the appearance of the generated objects, consistent
with too few tokens being updated according to the target request.

Taken together, the sweep indicates that the deployed 11-layer window is conservative: it sits where
quality is flat and most of the speedup is already realized, and a wider window of roughly 20 layers
is both slightly faster and slightly better on this pair. We retain the default because this evidence
rests on a single request--cache pair and a single seed, and because VBench dynamic degree is
constant at $1.0$ across all arms and therefore carries no signal in a single-video evaluation. Since
the region-update window and outer sparse connectivity share one partition of the backbone, the two
mechanisms should be re-tuned jointly on the request level rather than in isolation; we leave this to
future work.
